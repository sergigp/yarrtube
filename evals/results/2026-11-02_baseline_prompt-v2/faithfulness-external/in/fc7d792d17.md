This YouTube video explores the surprising connection between compression and training large language models, starting with the 2002 paper “Language Trees and Zipping.” The core concept is cross-entropy, which measures the difference between a model’s predicted probability distribution and the true distribution of data, effectively quantifying how well a compression scheme performs in a different context. This principle is applied to pre-training and distillation, where models are trained to minimize this cross-entropy loss, ultimately leading to a model that’s optimized for compression and, by extension, intelligence. Finally, the video introduces KL divergence as a related measure of distribution difference, highlighting its role in quantifying the wastefulness of suboptimal codes.
