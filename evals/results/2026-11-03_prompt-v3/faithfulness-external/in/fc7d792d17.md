This video explores the surprising connection between compression and training large language models, starting with the 2002 paper “Language Trees and Zipping.” The core concept is using cross-entropy, a measure of the difference between two probability distributions, to train models. Initially, cross-entropy arises from analyzing how well a compression algorithm performs on different text patterns, effectively quantifying the information content of the data. Ultimately, this approach reframes language model training as a compression task, offering a novel perspective on model development and highlighting the surprising link between intelligence and efficient data representation.
